# Zelftests voor burn-out, depressie, angst en slaap

Onderzocht op 11 oktober 2026 met vier onderzoekssporen en een onafhankelijke controle per spoor. Doel: een kleine set gevalideerde, gratis vragenlijsten in het Nederlands die Remi voor het bezoek aan de huisarts kan invullen. Het zijn screeningsinstrumenten, geen diagnose.

## Samenvatting (Engels, voor Giulia)

Yes. There are reliable, free questionnaires in Dutch that Remi can fill in before his GP visit on 16 October. Together they take about 15 minutes.

1. Burnout Assessment Tool (BAT). It was made at KU Leuven and its scores are compared against Flemish workers. It measures burnout complaints.
2. PHQ-9. This is the standard 9-question depression screen and covers the past two weeks.
3. GAD-7. These are 7 questions on worry and anxiety, which often come together with burnout.
4. Optional: the Epworth sleepiness questionnaire. Exhaustion and being late for work can also come from a sleep problem.

These are screening tools, not a diagnosis. A high score means "discuss this with the GP". A low score does not rule anything out. The burnout test cannot tell burnout apart from depression. In heavy workshop work, tiredness can also have physical causes, such as thyroid problems, anaemia, sleep apnoea or welding fumes, and only the GP can check those.

Remi should fill the questionnaires in himself, honestly, on a quiet day. He should then bring his answers and scores to the GP. A five-day sleep and energy diary and a short list of questions will help even more.

One safety rule: question 9 of the PHQ-9 asks about thoughts of death or self-harm. If he answers anything other than "not at all", do not wait until the 16th. Contact the GP that day or call the Zelfmoordlijn on 1813 (free, 24/7). In immediate danger, call 112.

## Aanbevolen

### Burnout Assessment Tool, core 23-item version (BAT-23), official Flemish-Dutch work-related version (KU Leuven: Schaufeli, De Witte, Desart)

- Meet: Work-related burnout complaints in 4 core parts:
- exhaustion (8 items)
- mental distance (5 items)
- cognitive impairment (5 items)
- emotional impairment (5 items)
An optional block of 10 secondary-symptom items covers psychological distress (sleep problems, tension, worrying) and psychosomatic complaints (headache, chest and muscle pain). It does NOT measure depression.
- Waarom: It is the only burnout questionnaire we found that meets all of these:
- free
- in Dutch (Flemish is the original language)
- developed and normed on Flemish workers
- has clinical cut-offs tested against diagnosed burnout patients, including Belgian patients

Belgian prevention services use it too (IDEWE, Securex), and the Flemish 'Werkbaar werk' platform explains it. The wording is simple and it takes about 5 minutes. It replaces the paid MBI/UBOS.
- Hoe: Download the official Flemish-Dutch work-related questionnaire: https://burnoutassessmenttool.be/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/BAT-Vlaams.pdf
The Dutch manual is here: https://burnoutassessmenttool.be/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/Gebruikshandleiding-BAT-versie-2.0.pdf
Neither link could be opened during this research (the site was blocked), so open them yourself first and check that they load.

We found no official interactive online test. Avoid third-party online copies (Jotform, coaching sites, and similar): at least one uses made-up score bands and asks for an e-mail address.

Use the WORK-related version, because he is still working.

Scoring:
- Answer the 23 core items: Nooit=1, Zelden=2, Soms=3, Vaak=4, Altijd=5.
- Add up the answers and divide by 23 to get the average.
- Also note the average of each of the 4 parts.
- Optionally fill in the 10 secondary-symptom items as well (about 10 minutes in total).

Shorter option: the BAT-12. It uses 12 of the same items; the manual lists which ones. It takes about 3 minutes.
- Interpretatie: The score is the average answer, from 1 to 5.

BAT-23 total, clinical cut-offs (Schaufeli et al. 2023; Flemish, Dutch and Finnish workers compared with diagnosed burnout patients):
- 1.00-2.58: green (no raised risk)
- 2.59-3.01: orange (at risk of burnout)
- 3.02 or higher: red (very high risk, severe burnout complaints)

BAT-12 total, if he uses the short version:
- below 2.54: green
- 2.54-2.95: orange
- 2.96 or higher: red
Use only the BAT-12 total, not its parts.

Red is a strong reason to discuss burnout with the GP. It is not a diagnosis. Green does not rule out a problem, especially on the BAT-12, which is less sensitive.

The manual also has Flemish statistical norms, which compare him with other Flemish workers ('high', 'very high'). Those are a different yardstick. A score can be 'high' compared with other workers and still be clinically green.

In heavy physical work, the exhaustion items can partly reflect physical tiredness. Bring the 4 part averages to the GP, but do not read them on their own; the mental-distance cut-off in particular is unreliable.

The BAT cannot separate burnout from depression or from physical causes of fatigue.

Certainty: the cut-off values were consistent across the research sources (the 2023 study, the manual table, a New Zealand Defence Force handout) and with the verifier's recollection. However, the full texts could not be opened during verification.
- Licentie: The official BAT site says the tool is non-proprietary and free to use without asking permission, on two conditions:
- the items are not changed
- this paper is cited: Schaufeli, Desart & De Witte (2020), IJERPH 17(24):9495
hrpraktijk.nl repeats this independently.

The verifier could not re-open the site. They noted that free use may be limited to non-commercial purposes; a private family app meets that condition. The PDF manuals carry an 'all rights reserved' notice, which most likely covers the manual text rather than the questionnaire.

In a private, non-commercial family app, reproduce the items word for word, with the citation. If in doubt, e-mail the KU Leuven authors.
- Mag in de app: ja, woordelijk en met bronvermelding
- Zekerheid: medium

### Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9), official Belgian-Dutch Pfizer translation ('PHQ-9 Dutch for Belgium')

- Meet: How severe depressive symptoms have been over the past 2 weeks. The 9 items follow the DSM criteria for major depression. Item 9 asks about thoughts of being better off dead or of self-harm. An unscored 10th question asks how much the problems get in the way of work, home life and contact with people.
- Waarom: The BAT does not detect depression, and depression needs its own assessment and treatment. The PHQ-9 is the best-studied short depression screen. It is free and has an official Belgian-Dutch text. GPs know it, and Sciensano uses it in Belgian health surveys. It takes about 3 minutes and includes a built-in safety question.
- Hoe: Use the official Belgian-Dutch PDF, hosted by the European Cystic Fibrosis Society: https://www.ecfs.eu/sites/default/files/general-content-files/working-groups/Mental%20Health/PHQ9_Dutch%20for%20Belgium.pdf
The Netherlands-Dutch version is from Tilburg University: https://www.tilburguniversity.edu/sites/default/files/download/Nederlandse%20PHQ9.pdf
Neither PDF could be opened during this research, so open it yourself and check that it shows the Pfizer attribution footer.

Print it and circle one answer per question about the past 2 weeks. Add up questions 1-9; question 10 is not counted.

There is no official interactive online version. Do the GAD-7 straight after it; it uses the same answer format.
- Interpretatie: Total of questions 1-9 (0-27), using the Kroenke 2001 severity bands:
- 0-4: none or minimal
- 5-9: mild
- 10-14: moderate
- 15-19: moderately severe
- 20-27: severe

A score of 10 or more is a POSITIVE SCREEN: discuss it with the GP. It does not mean 'probable depression'. In the large Levis 2019 meta-analysis, a cut-off of 10 had about 88% sensitivity and 85% specificity. Even so, many people who score 10 or more turn out not to have major depression when interviewed.

In one Dutch GP study, the cut-off of 10 missed about half of cases. So a score of 7-9, together with a clear effect on daily functioning, is also worth raising.

If question 10 is answered 'Erg moeilijk' or 'Extreem moeilijk', that points to impaired functioning.

Items 3 (sleep), 4 (tiredness) and 5 (appetite) can be pushed up by physical exhaustion from heavy work, or by medical causes. Note which items drove the score.

Item 9 (thoughts of death or self-harm): ANY answer above 0 means contacting the GP or the Zelfmoordlijn the same day, whatever the total (see safety). A 0 on item 9 does not rule out risk.
- Licentie: Copyright Pfizer Inc., with a blanket permission ('no permission required to reproduce, translate, display or distribute'). The verifier corrected the research on one point: this is permission from the copyright holder, not public domain.

It can be reproduced in a private app on these conditions:
- use the official text word for word
- do not change the scoring
- keep the attribution: 'developed by Drs. Robert L. Spitzer, Janet B.W. Williams, Kurt Kroenke and colleagues, with an educational grant from Pfizer Inc.'
- Mag in de app: ja, woordelijk en met bronvermelding
- Zekerheid: high

### Generalized Anxiety Disorder-7 (GAD-7), official Belgian-Dutch Pfizer translation ('GAD7_Dutch for Belgium')

- Meet: How severe anxiety and worry symptoms have been over the past 2 weeks. It was built for generalized anxiety disorder, and it also gives a moderate signal for panic, social anxiety and PTSD.
- Waarom: Worry, tension and anxiety often come together with overspanning and burnout. Anxiety is treated differently from burnout, so it helps the GP to see it measured. The GAD-7 uses the same answer format as the PHQ-9, so it adds only about 2 minutes. It is free and has an official Belgian-Dutch text. A Dutch web version has been validated (Donker et al. 2011).
- Hoe: Use the official Belgian-Dutch PDF: https://www.ecfs.eu/sites/default/files/general-content-files/working-groups/Mental%20Health/GAD7_Dutch%20for%20Belgium.pdf
The Netherlands-Dutch version: https://www.tilburguniversity.edu/sites/default/files/download/Nederlandse%20GAD-7.pdf
The ECFS index page: https://www.ecfs.eu/mental_health-tools/anxiety_screening_instrument
Open the PDF yourself first, because it could not be opened during this research.

Fill it in straight after the PHQ-9 and add up the 7 items.
- Interpretatie: Total score (0-21):
- 0-4: minimal
- 5-9: mild
- 10-14: moderate
- 15-21: severe

A score of 10 or more is the standard point for further evaluation by the GP. In the original study (Spitzer 2006) this cut-off had about 89% sensitivity and 82% specificity for generalized anxiety disorder. Some guidance uses 8 to 10.

Restlessness, irritability and poor sleep from overwork can raise the score. The GAD-7 cannot separate a stress reaction or overspanning from an anxiety disorder; that is the GP's job.
- Licentie: Same terms as the PHQ-9: copyright Pfizer with a blanket permission to reproduce, translate, display and distribute. Use the official text word for word, without changes, and keep the attribution to Spitzer, Williams, Kroenke and colleagues (educational grant from Pfizer Inc.).
- Mag in de app: ja, woordelijk en met bronvermelding
- Zekerheid: high

### Epworth Sleepiness Scale (ESS), optional

- Meet: Daytime sleepiness: the chance of dozing off in 8 everyday situations, such as reading, watching TV, being a passenger in a car for an hour, sitting after lunch, or sitting in a car stopped in traffic. Each situation is scored 0-3, for a total of 0-24.
- Waarom: Why it is worth adding:
- He has long-lasting exhaustion and is increasingly late for work. Sleep problems (sleep apnoea, insomnia, a late body clock) are a key alternative explanation, and the burnout and depression tests do not pick them up.
- It takes 2 minutes.
- It helps the GP tell 'sleepy, dozes off' (which points to a sleep problem) from 'exhausted but not sleepy' (which points more to stress, burnout or depression).
- Dozing off at a press brake, while welding or while driving is a safety issue.
- Hoe: These Dutch forms were seen only in search results; open them to confirm they load:
- Belgian hospital Sint-Trudo (Sint-Truiden): https://www.sint-trudo.be/sites/default/files/public/mcm_plus/00713-NL.pdf
- Groene Hart Ziekenhuis sleep-centre intake form (Netherlands): https://ghz.nl/media/xswp0y33/vragenlijst-watchpat.pdf
Alternatively, ask the GP for it on 16 October. Do not copy it into the family app.

If snoring is an issue, he can also fill in the Dutch STOP-Bang (Groene Hart Ziekenhuis: https://www.ghz.nl/media/rvcjzssr/stop-bang.pdf). Someone in the household should report on snoring and breathing pauses, and his neck should be measured.
- Interpretatie: A total above 10 means excessive daytime sleepiness and is a reason to discuss sleep testing with the GP.

The developer's (Johns) bands:
- 0-5: lower normal
- 6-10: higher normal
- 11-12: mild excessive sleepiness
- 13-15: moderate excessive sleepiness
- 16-24: severe excessive sleepiness
The verifier corrected the research here: use these bands, not the GPnotebook bands.

If he dozes off while driving or at machines, raise it urgently and avoid those situations until he has been assessed.

On STOP-Bang: a tired man who snores will screen 'high risk' almost automatically because of how it is scored. Treat a positive result as a reason to ask the GP about sleep, not as proof of sleep apnoea. Witnessed breathing pauses are the most telling item.

No Dutch psychometric validation of the ESS was found.
- Licentie: Copyright Murray W. Johns. Per the American Thoracic Society, it is free for an individual to use for himself; organisations and apps need a licence through Mapi Research Trust. This could not be re-checked during verification.

Filling in a hospital's public form for personal use is fine. Do NOT reproduce the items in the family app; link to the hospital form instead.
- Mag in de app: nee, enkel een link en scorehulp
- Zekerheid: medium

## Niet aanbevolen

- Maslach Burnout Inventory (MBI) and its Dutch version, the Utrechtse Burnout Schaal (UBOS): Paid and copyrighted (Mind Garden; Pearson Benelux). There are no public cut-offs suitable for scoring it yourself, and the Dutch norms are described as dated. Free 'MBI tests' online are usually unlicensed copies with made-up score bands. Its own developers built the BAT as its successor.
- Beck Depression Inventory (BDI-II-NL): Paid and copyrighted (Pearson). It offers no advantage over the free PHQ-9 for screening at home. Free online copies are unlicensed.
- Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale (HADS): Licensed through GL Assessment and Mapi Research Trust, with fees. There is no free official Dutch self-test, and it adds nothing over the PHQ-9 plus GAD-7 for a working adult.
- Oldenburg Burnout Inventory (OLBI): Free, but it has no Belgian norms, no validated cut-offs, and no openly published Dutch text. Its mix of positively and negatively worded items can confuse a lay person. Because it measures physical exhaustion, it may overestimate burnout in heavy manual work.
- Copenhagen Burnout Inventory (CBI): No validated Dutch or Flemish version and no Belgian norms. Its client-related scale does not fit workshop work.
- Kessler K10: Free, but no official Dutch item text was verified. Its single distress score does not separate depression, anxiety and stress.
- 4DKL as a home test built into the family app: It is a useful questionnaire, but it is copyright Dr B. Terluin and free only for non-commercial use in health care and research. It has 50 items and is meant to be interpreted by a clinician, and its anxiety cut-offs conflict between sources. Ask the GP to give it, or bring the NHG paper form; do not reproduce the items in the app.
- Unofficial online BAT copies (Jotform, coaching sites, and similar): They are not the official version. At least one uses non-official score bands (below 2.0 / 2.0-3.5 / above 3.5) and asks for an e-mail address. Score the official PDF yourself using the official cut-offs.
- BAT4 (4-item version) and PHQ-2 on their own: The BAT4 is meant for screening groups or organisations, not individuals. The PHQ-2 misses about a quarter or more of depressions and has no suicide question; the full PHQ-9 already contains it.
- Insomnia Severity Index (ISI): Copyrighted (Morin, through Mapi), with no official free Dutch form and no Dutch validation found. Record the same aspects in the sleep diary instead, or ask the GP or a psychologist to give it.
- Fit in je hoofd online test: A free Flemish wellbeing campaign test, but the instruments behind it are unknown. The sources found point to an old site (oud.fitinjehoofd.be) and a 2008 rework, so it may be outdated. It is not a screener a GP interprets with cut-offs. Only use it after checking that it is still current.
- Commercial 'burnout' or 'fatigue' blood panels: Testing everything mainly produces false alarms (about 40% chance of at least one 'abnormal' result across 10 tests in a healthy person). Let the GP choose a limited, targeted panel.
- Xerius/BloomUp burnout test: Only for self-employed Xerius members; Remi is an employee. The instrument behind it is not disclosed.

## Veiligheid

The scores themselves are not emergencies, but some answers are.

1) SAME DAY: do not wait for 16 October if any of these apply:
- Remi answers anything other than 'Helemaal niet' on PHQ-9 question 9 (thoughts that he would be better off dead, or of hurting himself in some way).
- He says anything like that.
- He talks about having no way out.
- The family is worried about his safety.

A 0 on question 9 does not rule out risk, so take spoken signals seriously too. If the questionnaires go into the family app, any answer above 0 on item 9 must show an immediate help message, not just a number.

Who to contact:
- Zelfmoordlijn 1813: free and anonymous. The phone line is open 24/7. There is also a chat on zelfmoord1813.be, with limited hours. Family members who are worried can call too.
- His GP: call the practice during the day and ask to be seen sooner.
- Evenings, nights and weekends: the huisartsenwachtpost (out-of-hours GP service) on 1733.
- Immediate danger (a concrete plan, means at hand, or an attempt): call 112 or go to the hospital emergency department, and do not leave him alone.
- Tele-Onthaal 106: free, anonymous, phone 24/7, for when he simply needs to talk.

2) CALL THE GP PRACTICE TO ASK FOR AN EARLIER APPOINTMENT (practical advice, not a guideline rule) if:
- the PHQ-9 total is 20 or more, or
- the PHQ-9 is 15-19 and he can hardly function.

Also call earlier for these physical warning signs:
- dozing off while driving or at machines: stop driving and operating the press brake until he has been assessed
- chest pain, shortness of breath or fainting
- unexplained weight loss, fever or night sweats
- blood in stool, urine or vomit
- new tremor, weakness, or trouble speaking or walking
- severe headache
- headache, dizziness or confusion during work that clears up away from the workshop (possible carbon monoxide): leave the area and get checked

3) A red BAT score, or a PHQ-9 or GAD-7 of 10-19 with no safety concerns, is not an emergency. Bring the results on 16 October.

The numbers 1813, 106, 112 and 1733 are well established, but they could not be re-checked live during this research, and the chat hours were not verified. Confirm them on zelfmoord1813.be and tele-onthaal.be and save them in his phone.

## Voorbereiding huisarts

- Remi fills in the BAT, PHQ-9, GAD-7 and (optionally) the Epworth himself on one quiet day between 12 and 14 October. He answers honestly and alone, about his own experience, and writes the date on each form. Family should not fill them in for him or push him. He brings the completed forms with each answer, plus the totals, to the GP. Ideally a trusted person is around afterwards.
- From tonight until 15 October, keep a short daily diary:
- bedtime and estimated time he fell asleep
- night wakings, wake time and snoozing
- energy and mood at midday and in the evening (0-10)
- naps or dozing off
- coffee, energy drinks, alcohol and cannabis
- hours worked and overtime, and how heavy the work was
- hours spent welding, and whether there was fume extraction or a mask
- headache or dizziness during or after work
- whether he was late, and why
Also note whether weekends feel different.
- Write a one-page summary covering:
- when the exhaustion started and how it has changed
- what makes it better or worse
- how often he is late
- mistakes or near-misses at the machines
- the effect on home and social life
- mood: loss of interest, low mood, guilt, hopelessness
- physical complaints: weight change, fever or night sweats, breathlessness, palpitations, pain
- medication and supplements
- past illnesses
- family history: thyroid, diabetes, depression
- Describe the work concretely. He combines three roles: technical drawer, welding/bending operator and workshop lead. Note his hours, overtime, responsibility, physical load and any conflicts, since these are classic burnout drivers.
- Describe his welding exposure for the GP and occupational doctor:
- processes (for example MIG/MAG and stick welding)
- metals (carbon steel, stainless, galvanised, painted)
- whether there is local fume extraction
- whether he wears a respirator
- whether he works in enclosed spaces
- whether co-workers have similar complaints
- Questions to ask the GP:
- Is this overspanning, burnout, depression, or a mix?
- Which blood tests do you advise, and why?
- Do I need a sleep assessment?
- Could my work exposures play a role, and should I see the occupational doctor (arbeidsarts)?
- Should I reduce or stop work for now, and for how long?
- Is it safe for me to drive and operate machines?
- Can you refer me to, or suggest, a contracted first-line psychologist (eerstelijnspsycholoog)?
- When is the follow-up appointment?
- Ask whether the GP wants him to complete the 4DKL (Vierdimensionale Klachtenlijst). This Dutch GP questionnaire separates a stress or burnout reaction from depression, anxiety and physical complaints. The paper form is hosted by the NHG: https://www.nhg.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/4dkl_voor_nhg_2014_0.pdf. He can fill it in and hand it over unscored, but the GP should interpret it. FOD WASO (the federal labour ministry) also publishes an early-detection tool for burnout that the GP fills in during the consultation; it is not a self-test.
- Do not come fasting for blood tests unless the practice asks for it. Glucose for this purpose is usually measured without fasting, and he should not skip breakfast before a physical shift.
- Remi decides whether a family member comes along. The GP speaks to an adult patient with his consent. A trusted person can help remember what was said.
- If the GP signs him off sick:
- Inform the employer immediately (phone or e-mail).
- Hand in the certificate within the deadline in the arbeidsreglement (company work rules); if none is set, this is often 2 working days.
- Check the arbeidsreglement: smaller firms can require a certificate from the first day.
- The employer's certificate states the incapacity and how long it is expected to last, not the diagnosis.
Not re-verified this session; confirm with the ziekenfonds or employer.
- Separately from the GP, Remi can ask his employer's external prevention service (for example IDEWE, Mensura, Securex, Attentia, Liantis or Cohezio) for a confidential spontaneous consultation (spontane raadpleging) with the occupational doctor about work-related complaints. It is free for the employee and reportedly takes place within 10 working days. He can ask for the employer not to be informed.

## Lichamelijke oorzaken

- The usual first blood panel for long-lasting fatigue in Dutch and Belgian GP guidance (from secondary sources; medium confidence):
- haemoglobin (anaemia)
- CRP or BSE (inflammation); CRP is more common in Belgium
- glucose (diabetes)
- TSH (thyroid)
Present this as 'what the GP will likely consider', not as a checklist to demand.
- Tests the GP adds only when there is a reason:
- creatinine/eGFR (kidneys)
- ALAT (liver)
- ferritin (iron stores), if anaemia or iron deficiency is suspected. In a 28-year-old man, low ferritin makes the GP look for the cause, not just prescribe a supplement.
- vitamin B12 and vitamin D are not routine. Belgian reimbursement for vitamin D testing is reportedly restricted (unverified). Ask whether they are useful, but do not expect them.
- Avoid commercial 'burnout blood panels'. About 5% of healthy people fall outside the reference range on any single test. With 10 tests, the chance of at least one false 'abnormal' result is about 40%. Most people with fatigue have normal blood results (roughly 1 in 12 to 1 in 5 consultations end in a physical diagnosis). A normal result does not mean nothing is wrong.
- Sleep assessment: obstructive sleep apnoea, insomnia and a late body clock can all cause exhaustion and lateness.
- Bring the Epworth score; above 10 is a reason to discuss it.
- Optionally bring the Dutch STOP-Bang: a household member reports snoring and witnessed breathing pauses, his neck circumference is measured, and his height and weight are noted.
- On STOP-Bang, the original neck threshold is 40 cm; the developer's current version uses a 43 cm collar for men, so check which one the form uses.
- A positive STOP-Bang means 'discuss with the GP', not proof of sleep apnoea.
It is up to the GP whether a sleep study is needed.
- Basic examination to expect or ask about: blood pressure, weight/BMI, a general physical exam, and questions about alcohol, cannabis, caffeine and energy drinks.
- Welding fumes and manganese: small studies of welders in confined spaces found dose-related fatigue, low mood and headache. There is no well-validated blood test for manganese exposure. Tell the GP and occupational doctor which processes and metals he works with and what ventilation there is. Stress, sleep and mood are still the more likely causes.
- Carbon monoxide: CO2-shielded (MAG) welding in poorly ventilated spaces can produce CO, which causes headache, fatigue and dizziness. A useful clue is complaints that are worse on work days and better at weekends or on holiday. A blood COHb test is only informative within a few hours after a shift. In practice, a CO monitor and a ventilation check in the workshop are more useful.
- Other workshop factors to mention (general occupational-medicine knowledge, not verified this session):
- metal fume fever after welding galvanised steel: flu-like symptoms that settle within 1-2 days
- noise
- heat and dehydration
- solvents
- painted or coated metal
- musculoskeletal pain from lifting and press-brake work, which disturbs sleep
The occupational doctor can do spirometry (lungs) and audiometry (hearing). Ask for the results of any periodic health checks already done through the prevention service.

- Ziekenfonds (mutualiteit): Reimburses GP care, blood tests and psychologist sessions. If he goes on longer sick leave, the Terug Naar Werk-coördinator (return-to-work coordinator) and the adviserend arts (the ziekenfonds doctor) help with a gradual or adapted return. Contact: His own ziekenfonds (CM, Helan, Solidaris, Partena, Liberale, and so on).. Kost: Varies by ziekenfonds.
- Gezondheid en Wetenschap: Belgian evidence-based patient information (CEBAM) on burnout and fatigue, for the family to read. Contact: https://www.gezondheidenwetenschap.be/richtlijnen/burn-out and https://www.gezondheidenwetenschap.be/richtlijnen/vermoeidheid. Kost: Free.

## Correcties door de controle

- PHQ-9: the research said a score of 10 or more means 'probable major depression'. Corrected: 10 or more is a positive screen to discuss with the GP. Many people who score 10 or more do not have major depression when interviewed.
- PHQ-9 accuracy: the research attributed the 0.78/0.87 meta-analysis to Manea 2015. It is Moriarty, Gilbody, McMillan & Manea, Gen Hosp Psychiatry 2015, with Manea as senior author.
- Zuithoff 2010: the finding that a cut-off of 10 'missed about half of cases in Dutch primary care' comes from a single study that used a fully structured interview (CIDI) as the reference. Present it as one study's result, not a general fact. The advice to raise a score of 7-9 when functioning is clearly affected still stands.
- Levis 2019 BMJ: the figures of 0.88 sensitivity and 0.85 specificity apply to the subset of studies using semistructured interviews (29 studies). They are not the result for the whole dataset.
- PHQ-9 and GAD-7 licensing: the research called them 'without copyright restriction'. Corrected: they are copyright Pfizer, with a blanket permission to reproduce, translate, display and distribute. The practical conclusion is unchanged: free to reproduce word for word with attribution.
- GAD-7: the claim that Plummer 2016 recommends a single cut-off of 8 was softened. Cut-offs of 8 to 10 are used, and 10 is the standard.
- 4DKL depression: the '7 or more = 44-86% probability' threshold was not supported. Use the standard bands instead (3-5 moderately elevated, 6 or more strongly elevated).
- 4DKL anxiety cut-offs: the old 'above 3 / above 9' values were rejected. The two verifications then disagreed on the replacement: one gave above 3 / above 8 (that is, 4-8 and 9 or more), the other the current two-cut-off model of 8 and 13. No anxiety cut-off is given here; the GP interprets the 4DKL.
- Copenhagen Burnout Inventory: seven items use the intensity answer format, not two.
- UBOS clinical criterion values filled in from the literature: exhaustion 2.20 or more, plus cynicism 2.00 or more or professional efficacy 3.66 or less. Not live-verified, and not relevant because the UBOS is licensed.
- BAT licence: 'free without any restriction' was softened to 'free, unaltered and cited; possibly limited to non-commercial use'. A private family app meets those conditions.
- Epworth Sleepiness Scale: the 'above 10' threshold stands, but the severity bands from GPnotebook were replaced by the developer's (Johns) bands: 0-5, 6-10, 11-12, 13-15, 16-24.
- STOP-Bang: the neck item was 40 cm in the original version. The developer's current version uses a 43 cm (17 inch) collar for men.
- STOP-Bang: under the refined scoring rule, a tired man who snores screens 'high risk' almost automatically. A positive result therefore means 'discuss with the GP', not 'likely sleep apnoea'.
- HADS: the 'experimental, research only' label for the Dutch version conflicts with the published Spinhoven 1997 validation and was set aside. The conclusion not to use the HADS is unchanged.
- Sick pay for arbeiders (blue-collar workers): '30 days of guaranteed wage, then 60% from the ziekenfonds' was corrected from the project's own earlier research:
- days 1-7: 100% from the employer
- days 8-14: 85.88% from the employer
- days 15-30: the ziekenfonds pays 60% of the capped wage, with an employer supplement on top
- from day 31: 60% of the capped wage from the ziekenfonds
This correction comes from an internal source, not an independent check.
- Confidence lowered from high to medium for the FOD WASO detection tool and the HGR advice 9339. The claim that Flemish GPs routinely use the 4DKL is an assumption without a source.
- Eerstelijnspsycholoog: the 'function 2' label for first-line support may be wrong and was dropped.
- Fasting for blood tests: glucose for a fatigue work-up is usually measured without fasting. Let the GP decide; he should not skip breakfast before a physical shift.
- How often a physical cause is found: the 20% (HenW) and 8% (NTVG) figures use different definitions. Present them as roughly 1 in 12 to 1 in 5, not as one number.

## Nog onzeker

- No claim was checked live during any of the four verification passes. The web-search budget was used up and every relevant host was blocked. Most facts rest on search-result snippets from the research phase, plus the verifiers' background knowledge.
- None of the key PDFs were opened in full: the official BAT questionnaire and manual (burnoutassessmenttool.be), the Belgian-Dutch PHQ-9 and GAD-7 (ecfs.eu), and the Dutch Epworth forms (Sint-Trudo, Groene Hart Ziekenhuis). The family should open them and confirm they load and contain what is described.
- The exact Belgian-Dutch PHQ-9 and GAD-7 wording was not verified, in particular PHQ-9 items 6, 8 and 9 and the instruction line. Copy them from the PDF before using them in the app.
- No official interactive online BAT test was confirmed. An NTNU scorer at theburnout.app was reported only through a press-release reprint; it is unverified and unofficial.
- BAT details not confirmed:
- the exact licence terms (non-commercial limitation; the 'all rights reserved' notice in the manual)
- the Belgian-specific BAT-12 red cut-off
- the exhaustion-subscale cut-offs for Belgium (3.06 and 3.31, dropped)
- the '10% false positives' figure (dropped)
- the exact Flemish statistical norm bands (possibly five bands, not four)
- No Flemish validation or Flemish norms were found for the PHQ-9, GAD-7, 4DKL or ESS, and no Dutch psychometric validation of the ESS. It is unknown whether the Domus Medica depression guideline (2017) recommends the PHQ-9 or the 4DKL. No Domus Medica burnout guideline was found. The latest NHG overspanning/burnout standard located is from 2018.
- 4DKL: the anxiety cut-offs conflict between sources, the official website was not reached, and the exact licence wording was not checked.
- Crisis and support numbers (1813, 106, 112, 1733, CAW 0800 13 500) and the chat hours were not re-checked live. 1813, 106 and 112 are well established; the CAW number is the least certain.
- First-line psychologist terms for 2026 still to confirm: first session free, the 8-session cap, the convention ending at the end of 2026, and the name of the Antwerp adult mental-health network. Supplementary reimbursement per ziekenfonds (for example CM) is unverified.
- Not researched or not verified:
- fatigue scales (Checklist Individuele Spankracht, Fatigue Assessment Scale, Need for Recovery)
- the Dutch-validated Holland Sleep Disorders Questionnaire
- AUDIT-C alcohol screening
- the Consensus Sleep Diary
Any of these could add to the package after checking licences and cut-offs.
- Employment details for the sick-leave rules: whether Remi is an arbeider or a bediende (the project files assume arbeider, joint committee PC 111). The 2026 rules on sick leave and return to work differ between the project's earlier research and this research; reconcile them before relying on them.
- Data inconsistency in the project: research/01-arbeidsrecht-uitstap.md gives Remi's age as 20, while the task says 28. It does not change these recommendations, but it should be corrected.
- Fit in je hoofd: whether the test is still online in October 2026, and which instruments it uses, is unknown.
